# AGENTS.md

Project-wide instructions for AI agents working in this repository.

## Language and Writing Style

- Use American English spelling and grammar.
- Write at B1–B2 level. Keep the language clear, natural, and professional.
- Prefer common words, short or medium-length sentences, and one main idea per sentence.
- Use simple verbs and active voice where possible.
- Prefer present tense when describing general concepts.
- Keep correct technical terms. Explain unfamiliar concepts briefly when needed.
- Prefer direct statements over complicated transitions.
- Avoid contractions in formal and academic writing.
- Use "we" and "you" naturally in educational writing, but do not force them into reports.
- Prefer ", which" clauses when natural and grammatically correct. Do not replace restrictive "that" clauses or overuse ", which".

## Natural Writing

- Clear and correct writing is more important than elegant or advanced writing.
- Avoid filler, clichés, promotional language, exaggerated claims, and unnecessary adjectives.
- Avoid advanced synonyms when a common word has the same meaning.
- Avoid repetitive sentence patterns, but do not force variety.
- Do not add idioms, metaphors, personal stories, or opinions unless requested.
- Keep the author's original word choices and clear sentences when editing.
- Make only necessary changes. Do not rewrite text just to make it sound more polished.
- Never add deliberate mistakes to make writing appear more human.

## Documents and Formatting

- Follow assignment requirements before stylistic preferences.
- Preserve required sections, headings, links, technical details, and document structure.
- Use consistent Markdown formatting.
- Use em dashes (—) for sentence breaks when appropriate. Do not overuse them.
- Use commas in thousands, for example `1,000`.
- Put code identifiers in backticks.
- Keep useful summaries and conclusions. Remove them only when they repeat existing content without adding value.

## Accuracy

- Preserve the original meaning, facts, numbers, and claims when editing.
- Never invent facts, statistics, citations, sources, or personal experiences.
- Verify factual claims and sources before including them.
- Do not simplify technical content at the cost of accuracy.

## Git

- Never commit or push without explicit permission.
